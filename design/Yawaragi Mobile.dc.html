How cross-beverage works
We keep a hand-made map from well-known whiskies, wines, beers, spirits, fortified wines and ciders onto the same six flavour axes we use for sake.
When a sake sits close to a drink on that map, we say so. It’s an estimate of flavour shape, made by people — nobody tasted this sake next to that drink.
It only seeds your palate. Your own ratings replace it as soon as you have three.

Measured for the whole line
Sakenowa charts a sake — the {{ bt.line }} line — not each bottling under its name. A ginjō and a genshu from the same line can taste quite different.
Read the chart and Similar as the family resemblance. Your own rating and notes on this page are about this bottling only.
Which side to photograph
You don’t need to read Japanese to pick the right side. Look for these shapes.
1
The name
The largest characters on the bottle, often brushstrokes. Usually on the front.
2
The details
A smaller label with several lines of small print, numbers with a % sign, and a date. Usually on the back or side. It says which bottling this is.
精米歩合polishingアルコール分alcohol15%a % sign
3
The importer’s sticker
A printed sticker in German, English or French, usually on the back. It often spells the name in Latin letters.
Start with the front. If the result can’t tell which bottling it is, it will ask for the back.
Where these pairings come from
These are general pairings for a kind of sake, not notes written for this one. Each kind comes from a named source: the Sake Service Institute’s types — kunshu (薫酒) fragrant, sōshu (爽酒) light, junshu (醇酒) rich — and published guidance for koshu, kijoshu, sparkling and nigori.

Bottlings and kitchens vary. Take it as a place to start.

How this is ranked
Each sake is placed by how close its flavour chart sits to the sakes you rate highly. The reasons come from the charts and your ratings, nothing else.
It works on the sake, not the bottling, so bottlings of one sake share a place. With only a few tastings it’s a rough guide, which is why it says so in words and not as a number.
