Turn 4 · colour, quieter
One hue, almost no chroma
Your three notes are one note: the ground and the accent were arguing. So the rule changed — ground and accent now sit in the same hue family and only a few steps apart in value, at a fraction of the chroma. The amber panel is gone with them: colour appears as a 2px mark and the star row, never as a fill. That is closer to how a sake label actually works, and it is what the Edo greys were for.
4a keeps your turquoise instinct but drops it into the ground's own family; 4b goes almost colourless — a tea-room grey-green; 4c keeps the seal idea from 3c with the heat taken out.

Turn 3 · colour, properly
Three answers out of the Japanese tradition
Every colour below is a named traditional colour, not an invented “Japanese-looking” hue. Edo sumptuary law pushed townspeople into indigo and into the greys and browns known as forty-eight browns, a hundred greys — restraint as taste, which is the same instinct the app needs. Indigo alone ran from pale 浅葱 asagi to near-black 褐色 kachi, so a blue-turquoise direction is not a modern affectation; it is the working colour of the period sake brewing belongs to.
All three are dark, because the room is dark. The question between them is how literal the water should be: 3a warm ground, cool accent — sake and its 和らぎ水; 3b fully in the water; 3c no water at all, ink and a single seal.
