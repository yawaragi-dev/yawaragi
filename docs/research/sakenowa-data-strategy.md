# Shipping Yawaragi: Is the Sakenowa Data Limitation Real, and What Should You Do?

## TL;DR
- **The core premise is wrong in an important way: the Sakenowa Data API is NOT frozen at 2024. It is actively maintained into mid-2026** — brand IDs now run up to 121,331, the `/rankings` endpoint returns `yearMonth: 202606` (June 2026), and operator Aiiro Systems Inc. shipped a feature update on June 28, 2026. What IS true is that Sakenowa's unique 6-axis flavor charts exist for only a subset of brands (an estimated ~1,500–1,600), so newly imported bottles reliably return name/brewery but often no flavor chart or match against a stale local cache.
- **You can ship now.** The right move is not a data-source pivot but a data-freshness fix plus honest UX: re-sync the live Sakenowa dataset, add a graceful "recognised, flavour profile coming soon" state, and layer a small curated delta database of the few hundred EU/US-imported SKUs that actually matter — not tens of thousands.
- **No single authoritative product-level sake API exists to replace Sakenowa.** JSS and NRIB publish brewery/award/process data, not a product API; Sakenote's API is closed; Sakenomy has no public API. The realistic authoritative supplements are (a) live Sakenowa re-sync, (b) importer/retailer catalogs (Tippsy/Palate Project, Tengu Sake), and (c) Rakuten's Ichiba Item Search API for current listings — combined with an LLM-assisted, human-reviewed ingestion pipeline.

## Key Findings

### 1. Sakenowa status
- **Sakenowa is run by Aiiro Systems Inc. (藍色システムズ株式会社)**, a private Tokyo company founded June 2019, representative Akira Ueda (上田明良). The service and the "Sakenowa Data Project" are live in 2026 (site footer: "© 2026 Aiiro Systems Inc.").
- **The data is current, not frozen.** The `/rankings` endpoint returns `yearMonth: 202606` (June 2026). The `/brands` endpoint contains brands with IDs climbing continuously to a maximum observed value of **121,331**, and the `/breweries` list extends to ID 1913 with brand records referencing even newer breweries — direct evidence of ongoing additions through 2025–2026. The news page's most recent post is dated **June 28, 2026** (a "Flavor Profile diagnosis" relaunch), with a steady 2026 cadence including app releases through May–June 2026.
- **Terms are extremely permissive.** Free, commercial and non-commercial use allowed, modification allowed; the only requirement is attribution (a visible link to https://sakenowa.com). No developer registration or API key is required. Contact is support@sakenowa.com.
- **There is no documented paid data tier or commercial licensing program.** Aiiro launched a consumer subscription "さけのわ+" (¥1,000/month) in April 2026, but that is an in-app upgrade for end users, not a data license. Fresher/fuller data or a formal partnership would have to be negotiated by email — which aligns with your plan to contact them.
- **Reconciling your observation ("only pre-2025"):** the most likely explanations are (a) your cached snapshot is a one-time 2024 pull that was never refreshed, and/or (b) you are matching against `/flavor-charts`, which only covers brands with enough user check-ins to derive a profile (estimated ~1,500–1,600 brands) and therefore lags new releases badly. The base `/brands` and `/breweries` tables are current; the flavor asset is the part that is sparse for new sake.

### 2. Alternative authoritative sources
- **Japan Sake and Shochu Makers Association (JSS / 日本酒造組合中央会):** The authoritative trade body (~1,347 sake-brewing member breweries; 1,691 total members as of June 2020). It runs a public brewery-search database at makers.japansake.or.jp, but that is brewery-level, not a product API. Notably, per a Gurunavi (株式会社ぐるなび) press release with JSS dated Aug 24, 2016, the two built a restaurant-facing 日本酒・焼酎データベース populated with brewery-provided data (brand, origin, ABV, rice, polishing ratio, taste, serving suggestions), multilingual in English, Traditional/Simplified Chinese and Korean — beginning with **140 breweries / 700 brands** trialed via prefectural guilds in Miyagi, Nagano, Kyoto, Kagoshima, Niigata and Akita. This is the most authoritative brewery-sourced dataset, but it is not openly available via API.
- **National Research Institute of Brewing (NRIB / 酒類総合研究所):** Publishes the National New Sake Appraisal (全国新酒鑑評会) award lists (authoritative but only for competition entries), a 清酒製造支援データベース (process-control data, not a product catalog), and label/price research reports. No product API and no open product dataset.
- **National Tax Agency (国税庁):** Publishes the nationwide commercial sake survey (全国市販酒類調査) with average SMV/acidity by grade — useful reference constants but not a product database. Industry sources citing the FY2020 (令和2年度) survey give average acidity of ~1.17 (general), ~1.35 (ginjo), ~1.38 (junmai), ~1.26 (honjozo); treat these specific constants as reported-but-unaudited (see Caveats).
- **Sakenote:** Had a "Database API," but new API applications are explicitly closed (新規お申し込み受付を終了). Dead end.
- **Sakenomy (MTI Inc. / Japan Craft Sake Company, supervised by Hidetoshi Nakata):** ~7,000+ brands with rich specs (SMV, acidity, yeast, rice, toji, polishing ratio, brewing water), label scanning, ~1,300+ breweries, and its own shop. No public API; would require partnership. It is a direct competitor to your concept.
- **SAKETIMES:** Media site with a 1,500+ brewery database; no API.
- **Sakepedia (Code for SAKE):** CC-BY open sake/brewery data, but it is community/user-generated and appears low-activity — out of scope given your authoritative-only, no-UGC constraint.
- **Rakuten Ichiba Item Search API:** Live and current (version 2026-04-01), free with an application ID. Returns itemName, itemCaption (often listing rice/polishing/SMV), price, shop, genre, and images for currently-sold products including brand-new and newly imported sake. This is the single best free source of *current* product listings, though data is unnormalised marketing text and requires parsing/dedup. ToS is affiliate-oriented and permits displaying item data with attribution.
- **Amazon Japan PA-API:** Requires affiliate sales qualification and has stricter caching/display rules; less attractive than Rakuten for a hobby project.
- **Importer/retailer catalogs (EU/US-side):** Tippsy (rebranded "Palate Project"), the largest US online sake retailer, is Shopify-based and per its own site offers **the largest selection of sake online, with more than 500 products**; its structured catalog carries grade, subcategory (nigori, nama, kimoto…), taste quadrant (Light & Dry etc.) and style tags — and founder Genki Ito has publicly said he wants richer brewery-spec-based curation. Tengu Sake (UK) supplies **over 39 award-winning sakes from 8–9 breweries** on WooCommerce, with its own pictogram flavour system — a strong signal of what is *available in your target EU market*, but a very small catalog. Both are partnership or (Shopify/WooCommerce JSON) candidates.
- **Untappd/Vivino-style platforms:** Beer/wine-centric with weak sake coverage and restricted APIs. Open Brewery DB is beer-only. Not viable for sake.

### 3. Own-database / hybrid feasibility
- **Scale is much smaller than feared for your market.** Japan has on the order of 20,000 sake products, but the universe *actually imported into the EU/US* is a small fraction. Tengu Sake carries ~39 SKUs; Tippsy carries 500+. The realistic curated delta of EU/US-relevant new labels per year is in the low hundreds of SKUs, not thousands or tens of thousands — a maintainable target for one person.
- **Market context justifies EU-first.** Per JSS figures (via Nippon.com, based on Japan's Ministry of Finance trade statistics), 2024 global sake exports rose 6.4% to **¥43.5 billion**, up 5.8% by volume to **31,000 kiloliters**, to a **record 80 countries**; exports to the United States leapt **25.9% to ¥11.4 billion** (volume +23.1% to 8,003 kL); and combined EU+UK exports rose **16.2% to ¥2.7 billion** at an average ¥1,400/liter, with Western Europe up ~18% year-on-year and roughly 2.5x over five years (from a small base, ~7% of export value). Growth is premium-skewed and restaurant/sommelier-led — favoring an education/flavour-profiling positioning.
- **Legal position for compiling from listings is workable but requires care.** Facts (brand name, brewery, SMV, polishing ratio) are not copyrightable in either the EU or Japan. In the EU/UK the sui generis database right (Directive 96/9/EC; UK Copyright and Rights in Databases Regulations 1997; 15-year term) protects *substantial extraction* from a database built on substantial investment — so wholesale copying of a competitor's catalog is risky, but compiling individual facts from many sources into your own structured DB is defensible. The bigger constraint is contract: per Ryanair v PR Aviation, a site can restrict scraping via its Terms even where no database right applies. Non-personal product data is outside GDPR. Japan's copyright law includes a broad text-and-data-mining/information-analysis exception (Art. 30-4). Practical rule: prefer official APIs (Rakuten) and brewery/importer pages; respect robots.txt and ToS; don't bulk-copy any single proprietary catalog.
- **An LLM-assisted, human-reviewed ingestion pipeline is a defensible "authoritative" approach** if you add provenance discipline: store the source URL, publisher, and fetch date for every field; prefer primary sources (brewery pages, importer product pages, JSS/Gurunavi-style brewery data); flag LLM-extracted fields as "unverified" until a human confirms; and show the source to users. This is materially different from UGC and is credible.
- **The 6-axis flavor chart is Sakenowa's genuinely hard-to-replace asset** because it is derived from NLP over thousands of Japanese user check-ins per brand (the six axes: 華やか/hanayaka, 芳醇/hojun, 重厚/juko, 穏やか/odayaka, ドライ/dry, 軽快/keikai) — you cannot reproduce it for a brand with no check-ins. You *can* approximate a coarse flavour vector for new sake from official specs: SMV and acidity map to the classic sweet–dry / light–rich (淡麗–濃醇) quadrant (there are published formulas using SMV and total acidity — e.g. 甘辛度 = 193593/(1443+SMV) − 1.16×total acid − 132.57; 濃淡度 = 94545/(1443+SMV) + 1.88×total acid − 68.54), and grade/polishing ratio/yeast/rice correlate with aromatic intensity. This is defensible only if clearly labelled as an *estimated* profile distinct from Sakenowa's review-derived chart, and it should not be presented as equivalent.

### 4. Product strategy options
- **A. Re-sync live Sakenowa + honest coverage UX (lowest effort, do this now).** Refresh from the live API, cache with a scheduled re-pull, and design a clear "recognised — flavour profile not yet available" state instead of a hard "no match." Pros: near-zero cost, keeps within permissive terms, immediately fixes most of the perceived staleness. Cons: still no flavour data for the newest bottles.
- **B. Sakenowa base + curated EU/US delta DB (recommended core).** Keep Sakenowa as the historical/flavour base and maintain a small curated table of EU/US-imported SKUs (from importer/retailer pages + Rakuten), with estimated flavour vectors clearly marked. Pros: directly solves the scan-a-new-import gap; scale is only hundreds/year. Cons: ongoing curation time.
- **C. Partnership/licensing.** Email Sakenowa (support@sakenowa.com) to ask about fuller/fresher data and coordination; separately explore a data/affiliate tie-up with an importer (Tengu in the UK, Tippsy/Palate Project in the US). Pros: authoritative, potential monetisation. Cons: depends on others; slow.
- **D. Reposition toward education/flavour-profiling of classic brands.** Lean into decoding taste for the ~1,500 well-profiled classics — matching the premium, sommelier-led nature of EU demand — so new-release coverage matters less. Pros: plays to Sakenowa's strength and the market's actual behaviour. Cons: less "new bottle tracker" appeal.

## Details
Yawaragi's dependency risk is lower than assumed: the free Sakenowa API is live, permissively licensed, and updated into 2026, so the immediate priority is engineering hygiene (re-sync + graceful degradation), not replacement. The genuine gap — flavour data for very new/newly imported bottles — is bounded by how few sake SKUs actually reach EU/US shelves. That makes a hybrid (Sakenowa base + a few-hundred-SKU curated delta fed by Rakuten's live API and importer catalogs, with LLM-assisted extraction and human review) both technically and legally feasible on a hobby budget (<€15/month). Sakenowa's review-NLP flavor chart remains the one asset you cannot fully reproduce; approximate it transparently from published specs rather than claiming parity.

## Recommendations
1. **Immediately (days):** Re-pull the live Sakenowa `/brands`, `/breweries`, `/flavor-charts`, `/brand-flavor-tags` endpoints and schedule a monthly re-sync. Add the required attribution link. Ship a "recognised, flavour profile coming soon" UX for brands lacking a chart. This alone likely resolves most of the "no match" complaints and materially de-risks launch.
2. **Weeks 1–4:** Stand up a lightweight curated delta table for EU/US-imported SKUs, seeded from Tengu Sake (UK) and Tippsy/Palate Project (US) plus Rakuten Ichiba Item Search API results, with per-field provenance (source URL + date) and an LLM-extraction + human-review step. Mark spec-derived flavour vectors as "estimated."
3. **In parallel:** Email support@sakenowa.com to (a) confirm update cadence/coverage and (b) ask about coordination or a fuller data feed; note attribution is already implemented. Separately sound out one importer for an affiliate/data tie-up.
4. **Positioning:** Lead with education and flavour-decoding of well-profiled classics (EU-first, premium/sommelier framing), with new-import scanning as a "we'll profile it" feature rather than a promise of instant full data.
5. **Thresholds that change the plan:** If Sakenowa's terms change or the API is withdrawn → accelerate option B/C. If your curated delta exceeds ~1,000 active SKUs or curation exceeds ~1 day/month → pursue a paid importer partnership or Sakenomy/Tippsy data deal. If users predominantly scan very new bottles (not classics) → prioritize the Rakuten-fed delta and estimated vectors over the historical base.

- Whether Sakenowa offers any paid/partnership data option could not be verified — none is published; this must be confirmed by email.

- Sakenomy, Tippsy, and importer partnership feasibility are inferred from public materials; none has a published open API.
